Start with the basics

Clinical trials, explained simply

What a trial is, what the phases mean, how research can help, and your rights as a participant.

🔬

What is a clinical trial?

A clinical trial is a planned research study involving people. Researchers study a medicine, treatment, test, device or care approach to learn about its safety, effects or how it compares with existing care. Some studies observe people without giving a new treatment.

📈

What do the phases mean?

Many studies of new medicines move through phases, although not every clinical study follows this sequence.

Phase I Usually checks safety, side effects and dose in a small group.

Phase II Looks for early evidence of benefit while continuing safety checks.

Phase III Studies a larger group and compares the treatment with standard care or another option.

Phase IV After approval, researchers learn more about use and longer-term effects.

🤝

How can participation help?

A participant may receive closer follow-up or access to an investigational option, but there can be risks and no guarantee of personal benefit. Well-run studies help healthcare teams understand safety, effectiveness and better ways to prevent, diagnose or treat illness. Taking part is a personal choice, not a duty.

🛡️

Your rights as a participant

Understand firstYou should receive clear information and have time to ask questions before agreeing.
Choose freelyYou can say no. You should not be pressured to join.
Leave the studyYou can usually withdraw; ask what this means for your ongoing care.
Ask about safetyAsk about risks, alternatives, costs, injury care and whom to contact.
PrivacyAsk how your information is protected and who can see it.
Consent is not a guaranteeSigning a consent form does not guarantee benefit or that you will receive the investigational treatment.

Why TrialMitra?

Clinical research should be easier to navigate.

Information about clinical studies can be technical, scattered and difficult to interpret. TrialMitra is a concept for making study information more approachable, multilingual and patient-centred.

Our goal: help people discover information and connect with study teams while respecting informed consent and patient choice.
